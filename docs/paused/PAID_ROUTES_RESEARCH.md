# Paid and licensed supply routes for passenger-view train footage

Research date: 2026-11-22. Web research only; no vendor was contacted, nothing was signed up for or bought.

**How to read the labels**

- **CONFIRMED (page)**: read from the vendor's own page via an automated page fetch. The fetch tool returns a machine summary of the page, so wording and figures should be re-checked by eye before any purchase.
- **CONFIRMED (snippet)**: taken from a search-result snippet of the vendor's own domain; the page itself could not be opened (HTTP 403).
- **INDICATIVE**: third-party article, tracker, forum or single freelancer listing.
- Pages that refused automated access (403): pond5.com, shutterstock.com/pricing, motionarray.com, help.elements.envato.com, help.artlist.io, fiverr.com. Figures for those are snippet-level at best.

## Headline findings

1. **Supply, not price, is the binding constraint for stock.** Subscription libraries are cheap (about $17-65/month) but the train-view clips seen are 5-60 seconds long and mostly generic countryside; city-identified passenger-view footage is sparse. No multi-minute cab-ride clip was found on any stock site, including Pond5.
2. **Subscription licences cover only works published while subscribed.** Storyblocks, Envato and Motion Array all tie the licence to projects completed/published during the paid term. New compilations and new livestreams made later need an active subscription, so the subscription is effectively permanent for this channel model.
3. **Per-clip stock is far outside budget** for 5 minutes of footage per destination (hundreds of dollars per destination even at the cheapest prices seen).
4. **Commissioning is the only route that reliably yields multi-minute, city-specific footage with a clean rights position**, at roughly $75-500 per destination depending on region. At $250/month that is about 1-3 destinations per month.
5. **YouTube risk is separate from licensing.** YouTube's policy says permission does not cure reused content, and its inauthentic-content policy targets templated, easily replicated videos. Stock footage is not named, but a channel of stock clips plus music fits the descriptions more closely than self-commissioned footage does.

## YouTube "reused content" and "inauthentic content" policy

Source: YouTube channel monetization policies, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en (read 2026-11-22 via page fetch; quotes are short extracts).

- Definition: "Reused content refers to channels that repurpose content that's already on YouTube or another online source" ... "without adding significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value."
- Permission does not cure it: "This policy applies even if you have permission from the original creator."
- Channel-level consequence: "monetization may be removed from your entire channel."
- Named non-monetisable examples include: "Content uploaded many times by other creators"; "Content downloaded or copied from another online source without any substantive modifications"; "Collections of songs from different artists (even if you have their permission)".
- Allowed example: "Edited footage with audio and visual effects on top of the video's reused content that demonstrates substantive editing"; and "we allow reused content if viewers can tell that there's a meaningful difference between the original video and your video."
- Inauthentic content (renamed from "repetitious content" on 2025-07-15): content "that looks like it's made with a template"; example: "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative".
- **Stock footage is not named anywhere on the page.** "Compilations" appears only in the July 2025 update note, as a format the reused-content policy reviews ("commentary, clips, compilations, and reaction videos").

Reading for this channel: licensed stock clips that other channels can also download fall close to "Content uploaded many times by other creators", and a 200-video library on one template with only the city changing is close to the inauthentic-content description. Commissioned, exclusive footage plus visible editing and destination-specific material lowers both risks. This is an interpretation; YouTube does not publish a ruling on stock-plus-music channels.

## Cost to reach 20 and 200 destinations: ESTIMATES

These are estimates, not quotes. Assumptions:

- Each destination needs **6 minutes of raw footage** (range 5-8) for a 5-minute segment.
- Stock clips average **20 seconds** (observed range 5-60 seconds), so 6 minutes needs about **18 clips** (12 if every clip is 30 seconds).
- Exchange rate about $1.27 per £1 where pounds are converted.
- Subscription rows assume the footage exists, which the catalogue evidence contradicts for most named cities.

One commissioned ride usually produces 30-120 minutes of footage, so a single shoot can cover several segments of one city or adjacent cities on one line, which would lower the per-destination figure.
