## YouTube: disclosing altered or synthetic content
Source: support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491 ("Disclosing use of GenAI content"), article body read in full.

- Rule: "We require creators to disclose AI-generated or altered content that seems realistic."
  "Creators don't need to disclose non-realistic content that's made with AI".
- Listed as not needing disclosure: "Using an AI-generated or altered animation of a missile in a fully
  animated video"; "AI generating or extending a backdrop to simulate a moving car".
- Listed as needing disclosure: "Alters footage of a real event or place"; "AI generated extra footage of a
  real place"; "Generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur". Each is tied to footage or realism.
- Consequence of not disclosing when required: creators who "consistently choose not to disclose" may get a
  label applied, or penalties including removal or suspension from the Partner Program. YouTube may also
  apply a label itself when its systems detect AI content.
- "Disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money."

**Reading for our videos:** fully illustrated, painterly, plainly not photographic, so they fall under
"non-realistic" and disclosure is not required as the page is written. Residual risk: the page does not
define "realistic", and our videos show real, recognisable places; YouTube could take a different view or
label a video itself. The owner chose no label (2026-11-22). The description states the artwork is
illustrated and not real footage, which addresses the "mislead the viewer" concern.

## OpenAI: ownership and use of generated images
Sources: openai.com/policies/services-agreement (effective 1 January 2026), /usage-policies
(effective 20 November 2026), /service-terms (updated 13 November 2026).

- Ownership, Services Agreement 4.1: "Customer ... owns all Output. OpenAI hereby assigns to Customer all
  OpenAI's right, title, and interest, if any, in and to Output", "to the extent permitted by applicable law".
- Commercial use: no clause forbids it; 4.3 makes the customer "solely responsible for all use of the Outputs".
- No general requirement to disclose that images are AI-generated was found in these three documents.
- 4.4: "Output may not be unique, and other users may receive similar content".
- Usage Policies: no use to "infringe on intellectual property rights of others"; no use of a person's
  likeness without consent "in ways that could confuse authenticity". Our art shows buildings, not people.
- Third-party claims: OpenAI's indemnity for API customers covers IP claims over Output, but excludes
  trademark claims from use "in trade or commerce" and Output that was "modified or combined". Our videos
  combine and modify the images, so assume **no indemnity**: any claim over a depicted building or mark is
  the owner's risk.
- Not read: OpenAI's image-generation documentation, which the Service Terms incorporate, and the consumer
  Terms of Use (not applicable to API use).
